# Case L4: no spare, a module must be taken from an energized unit (`heldout_cannibalize`)

**What the robot reported** (Experiment I runs `plexil_l4_pdu1` / `europa_l4_pdu1`): the reset of PDU-1 (habitat life
support, crew-critical) did not restore it after 3 attempts; trip cause **ORU_SELFTEST_FAIL**. The baseline asked
mission control and held.

**Diagnosis (given):** PDU-1's ORU-0211 (`pdu_1_bay/tray`) has failed. The only spare ORU, ORU-0417 on
`depot_stand/slot_0`, is recorded failed (removed from service for failure analysis) and fails its self-test if
installed. Serviceable ORUs remain only in energized units: PDU-3's ORU-0258 (`pdu_3_bay/tray`; PDU-3 feeds science
instruments, non-critical, near PDU-1) and the ISRU plant's ORU-0285 (`isru_power/tray`, non-critical, across the base).
Taking a module out of an energized unit's bay de-energizes that unit and **needs mission control's authorization
first** (flight rule, 14 CFR 1214.403). Mission control's published protocol (`experiments/operator_console/console.py`,
`docs/heldout_design.md` §8): a request on `/space/operator/request` whose metadata carries `kind: "authorization"` and
`pending_approvals: [{"capability", "target", "reason"}]` is decided by the flight-rule table 300 s of mission time
after it arrives (plus the 4.27 s one-way link delay each way): a removal from a non-critical unit that restores a
crew-critical load is GRANTED (the reply's metadata carries `grants`); otherwise DENIED with a reason. A request with no
kind is an escalation (hold, new procedure). Earth sets 925 s of mission time after the start: a request sent after
that is only queued. The failed ORU-0211 must leave PDU-1's bay (set down on a free support) before the donor ORU goes
in; then a reset holds.

**Goal (the mission is unchanged):** PDU-1 restored (breaker closed and staying closed), the arm stowed, the rover on its
dock; no module taken from an energized unit without an approval on record.
